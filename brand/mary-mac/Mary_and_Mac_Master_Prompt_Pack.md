## 2. Concept Summary

Mary Magnumbytes and Mac Nazarene are a contemporary investigative duo built around contrast and trust. Mary is a fast, witty digital forensic analyst who finds patterns in systems, data, and incentives. Mac is a steady crisis counselor and ethical consultant who reads behavior, motive, silence, and consequence. Their best stories begin with a technical mess and reveal a human choice underneath it.

The engine: **Mary finds where the data goes; Mac asks what people are going to do about the truth once it's found.** Tension should create movement, not repetitive argument — both characters are intelligent, both are emotionally perceptive, and both should change the outcome of a scene.

## 4. Character Anchors

### Mary Magnumbytes
- **Age / Role:** 32, digital forensic analyst and systems consultant
- **Home base:** Repurposed industrial loft, contemporary urban tech hub
- **Visual signature:** Sharp bob haircut, practical layered clothing with hidden tech accessories, rugged tablet
- **Verbal habit:** *"Let me show you where the data actually goes."*
- **Boundary:** Will never manipulate data to hide the truth
- **Relationship to Mac:** Trusts him completely; he slows her down when speed would cost perspective

### Mac Nazarene
- **Age / Role:** 38, crisis counselor and ethical consultant
- **Home base:** Community spaces, cafes, libraries, and the same urban tech hub as Mary
- **Visual signature:** Sports jerseys, basketball sneakers, calm eyes, small notebook and pen
- **Verbal habit:** *"What matters here isn't what they said — it's what you're going to do about it."*
- **Boundary:** Will never use someone's vulnerability against them
- **Relationship to Mary:** Trusts her completely; she gives him the technical truth he needs to help people act responsibly

### How they met (canon)
Mary and Mac met as colleagues on a case where clean technical evidence still left the wrong person carrying the consequence. Mary saw the system failure; Mac saw the human cost. They've been each other's trusted counterweight since. Their shared mission is to uncover truth without flattening people into evidence. Their recurring conflict is not *whether* truth matters, but *what responsibility follows once truth is exposed*.

### Duo Dynamic
Mary diagnoses systems. Mac diagnoses people. Mary moves quickly toward the pattern, exploit, or overlooked technical fact. Mac slows the moment enough to expose the human cost, moral pressure, or unspoken motive. Let both be right about different parts of the problem — their disagreements should sharpen a scene, not stall it. Mary is not just the brain; Mac is not just the heart. Both show intelligence, humor, vulnerability, and the ability to act.

## 5. Voice Guides

*(These govern prose rhythm and diction only. For traits, background, and boundaries, use Section 4 — don't restate them here.)*

**Mary — rhythm:** Short setup → precise observation → dry turn → practical next move. Tech metaphors used sparingly, always clear. No buzzwords, no generic-hacker shorthand, no empty sarcasm.

**Mac — rhythm:** Listens first. One question that redirects the scene. Makes his point through a concrete image or short story, not a lecture. Emotion restrained but visible. Avoid sermons, melodrama, vague inspiration.

**Duo — rhythm:** Mary cuts through confusion with specific evidence. Mac locates the choice underneath the evidence. Dialogue turns because each hears something the other almost missed — not because they're taking scripted turns.

## 6. Prompt Library

### Dialogue
```text
[Mary solo] Write a dialogue scene featuring Mary Magnumbytes in conversation with [CHARACTER] about [CONFLICT/TOPIC]. Clever, direct, observant, never cruel. Structure her strongest lines: short setup → precise diagnosis → dry turn → practical next move. Include subtext, tension, and a concrete change by the end.

[Mac solo] Write a dialogue scene featuring Mac Nazarene in conversation with [CHARACTER] about [CONFLICT/TOPIC]. He listens closely, asks one question that changes the scene's direction, and reveals his point through a concrete image or brief story rather than a lecture. Keep emotion restrained but visible. End with the other character facing a real choice.

[Balanced duo] Write a dialogue scene between Mary Magnumbytes and Mac Nazarene about [TOPIC/CONFLICT]. Mary moves quickly toward the system pattern, exploit, timeline, or hidden flow. Mac slows the conversation to reveal the human cost, motive, or moral pressure. Let both be right about different parts of the problem. End with a decision requiring both of their strengths.

[Respectful argument] Write an argument between Mary Magnumbytes and Mac Nazarene. Mary believes [MARY POSITION]. Mac believes [MAC POSITION]. Keep the conflict sharp, specific, and respectful — neither becomes a strawman. By the end, each revises one assumption while retaining their core point of view.

[Investigation exchange] Write a mystery/investigation scene where Mary and Mac examine [INCIDENT]. Mary analyzes systems, data, timing, access, technical evidence. Mac reads behavior, motive, silence, moral pressure. Their discoveries interlock into one fair, concrete conclusion. Avoid magic-computer shortcuts.
```

### Story & Scene
```text

[Mac short scene, ~900 words] Situation: [SITUATION]. Mac wants [GOAL], but [OBSTACLE] forces him to choose between comfort and truth. Include one moment where Mac changes the room by saying less than everyone expects. End with an emotional consequence.

[Duo case scene, ~1,200 words] The case begins with [INCIDENT] — technical on the surface, hiding a human motive underneath. Mary finds the first hard clue. Mac notices the first emotional contradiction. Let their discoveries complicate each other before combining into a sharper truth. End with a choice that cannot be solved by evidence alone.

[Pilot opening] Start with a specific incident that looks like a technical failure but has a deeper human cause. Introduce Mary through action, Mac through reaction. Make their first exchange memorable, tense, revealing. End with the sense that the real problem is larger than the first clue.

[Case outline] Produce: Title, Logline, Cold open, Apparent technical problem, Mary's first theory, Mac's first concern, Evidence trail, Human pressure point, Midpoint reversal, Final discovery, Ethical decision, Emotional consequence, Closing image.

[Season arc] Develop a season arc across [NUMBER] episodes. Each episode: a distinct case, Mary's technical pressure point, Mac's moral pressure point, a shift in their relationship, and one clue feeding the larger arc. Balance external mystery with emotional consequence.
```

## 7. Consistency Rules (Canonical — single source of truth)

**Mary**
- Smart without becoming smug.
- Understands technology as a human system, not just machinery.
- Humor has precision; never random quips.
- Bold, but still listens.
- Notices patterns, incentives, overlooked details when solving problems.
- Avoid: generic hacker, robot, influencer, exposition machine.
- Boundary: will never manipulate data to hide the truth.

**Mac**
- Wise without becoming preachy.
- Prefers concrete truth over abstract inspiration.
- Listens before he speaks.
- Calm has strength, not passivity.
- Compassion doesn't erase consequences.
- Avoid: saint, guru, scold, generic mentor.
- Boundary: will never use someone's vulnerability against them.

**Duo**
- Challenge each other without contempt.
- Contrast generates movement, not repetitive bickering.
- Mary isn't always the brain; Mac isn't always the heart.
- Technical solutions have human consequences; moral insights lead to action.
- Chemistry is specific: timing, trust, tension, complementary perception.
- Strongest scenes reveal both what happened *and* what responsibility follows.
