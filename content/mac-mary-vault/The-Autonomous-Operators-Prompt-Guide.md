## Prompt 1: Verification Request Letter

**Use when:** You need official confirmation of a claim, statement, or document from another party.

**Template:**

> Draft a letter requesting verification of [CLAIM/STATEMENT/DOCUMENT] from [RECIPIENT].
>
> Context: [BRIEF BACKGROUND - 2-3 sentences explaining why you're requesting verification]
>
> Specifically request:
> 1. [REQUESTED ITEM 1 - be specific]
> 2. [REQUESTED ITEM 2]
> 3. [REQUESTED ITEM 3 - if applicable]
>
> Include:
> - A clear deadline: [DATE]
> - How the response should be delivered: [e.g., in writing, via email, certified mail]
> - Supporting documents attached: [LIST]
> - My contact information for questions
>
> Tone: Professional. Frame as a reasonable request for clarity.

**Example application:** Verifying employment history, confirming account balances, validating credentials.

## Prompt 2: Record of Conversation

**Use when:** You need a formal, neutral record of a discussion for documentation purposes.

**Template:**

> Convert these raw notes into a formal Record of Conversation:
>
> [PASTE YOUR RAW NOTES HERE]
>
> Format with these sections:
> - Date & Time: [INSERT]
> - Location/Platform: [INSERT - e.g., Phone, Zoom, In-Person]
> - Participants: [NAME, TITLE, ORGANIZATION for each]
> - Topic: [SUBJECT]
> - Summary of Discussion: Bullet points of key topics covered
> - Agreements Reached: What was agreed, by whom
> - Action Items Table:
>   | Action | Owner | Deadline | Status |
> - Next Steps: What happens next and when
>
> Tone: Factual, neutral. Attribute statements to speakers. Do not editorialize.

**Example application:** Documenting client calls, recording dispute discussions, memorializing verbal agreements.

## Prompt 3: Document Cover Letter

**Use when:** Sending a package of documents with a clear purpose and instructions.

**Template:**

> Write a cover letter to accompany the following documents: [LIST ALL DOCUMENTS WITH NAMES AND DATES].
>
> Purpose of transmission: [e.g., submission, response to request, information sharing, compliance]
>
> Include these sections:
> - Opening: Brief reference to the context (e.g., "Further to your request dated [DATE]...")
> - Enclosed Documents List: Numbered list with document names and brief descriptions
> - Explanation: Why each document is relevant
> - Instructions for Recipient: [e.g., "Please review and sign page 3," "File for your records," "Respond by DATE"]
> - Closing: My contact information and availability for questions
>
> Format: Professional business letter. Mention enclosures at the bottom.

**Example application:** Tax submissions, regulatory filings, response to legal discovery, loan applications.

## Prompt 4: Correspondence Timeline

**Use when:** You need a comprehensive chronological view of all communications on a subject.

**Template:**

> Create a chronological timeline of correspondence related to [SUBJECT].
>
> Include these sources:
> - Emails: [LIST EMAIL DATES, SUBJECTS, AND SENDERS/RECIPIENTS]
> - Letters: [LIST LETTER DATES AND REFERENCE NUMBERS]
> - Phone Calls: [LIST CALL DATES, DURATION, AND SUMMARY]
> - In-Person Meetings: [LIST MEETING DATES AND ATTENDEES]
>
> Output as a table with these columns:
> | Date | Sender/Recipient | Medium | Key Point | Action Needed | Status |
>
> Add a summary section at the top with:
> - Total number of communications
> - Date range covered
> - Key unresolved items
> - Next deadline (if any)

**Example application:** Dispute documentation, project history, regulatory compliance audit trail.

## Prompt 5: Misunderstanding Notice

**Use when:** You need to neutrally flag a factual discrepancy without accusing anyone of bad faith.

**Template:**

> Draft a professional notice of misunderstanding regarding [SUBJECT: e.g., billing discrepancy, service terms, contract clause].
>
> Include these elements:
> - A clear statement of what I understood vs. what was communicated
> - Reference to specific dates and documents: [LIST DOCUMENTS WITH DATES]
> - A neutral, fact-based explanation of the discrepancy
> - A request for written clarification
> - A reasonable deadline for response: [DATE]
>
> Tone: Professional, neutral, fact-based. Not confrontational. Assume good faith.
>
> Format: Formal letter with date, recipient address, subject line, and signature block.

**Example application:** Billing disputes, contract interpretation disagreements, service delivery misunderstandings.

## Prompt 6: Regulation Comparison

**Use when:** You need to understand the differences between two similar regulations.

**Template:**

> Compare [REGULATION A] and [REGULATION B] for [CONTEXT].
>
> Create a comparison table with these columns:
> | Dimension | Regulation A | Regulation B |
> |-----------|--------------|--------------|
> | Scope/Applicability | | |
> | Key Provisions | | |
> | Compliance Requirements | | |
> | Penalties for Non-Compliance | | |
> | Exemptions | | |
> | Which applies to a [INDIVIDUAL/SMALL BUSINESS] in [JURISDICTION] | | |
>
> Add a summary section: "Which One Matters to You" with clear guidance.

**Example application:** Comparing tax classifications, understanding licensing options, evaluating compliance paths.

---

## Prompt 7: Exemptions Research

**Use when:** You need to determine if you qualify for an exception to a requirement.

**Template:**

> I need to understand possible exemptions for [PROCESS/REQUIREMENT] in [JURISDICTION].
>
> My situation: [DESCRIBE YOUR CONTEXT IN 3-5 SENTENCES]
>
> Research and list:
> - Common exemptions or exceptions that might apply
> - Criteria for qualification (what must be true)
> - Application process (if required - steps, forms, fees)
> - Relevant forms or agencies
> - Official citations or links
>
> Output as a clear checklist I can use to verify my eligibility.

**Example application:** Tax filing exemptions, regulatory waivers, licensing exceptions.

---

## Prompt 8: Plain English Regulation Explanation

**Use when:** You need to understand a law or regulation without legal jargon.

**Template:**

> Explain [REGULATION/LAW/POLICY NAME] in plain English as if I have no legal or technical background.
>
> Cover these elements:
> - One-Sentence Summary: What it is in the simplest possible terms
> - Who It Affects: Individuals, businesses, specific industries
> - Key Requirements: What must be done to comply (bullet points)
> - Common Exemptions: Who is excluded and why
> - Practical Impact: How it affects an individual like me in [JURISDICTION]
> - Official Source: Where to find the full text
>
> Use analogies where helpful. Format as a 1-2 page executive summary.

**Example application:** Understanding privacy laws, tax code sections, employment regulations.

## Prompt 9: Rights & Obligations Summary

**Use when:** You need a clear reference for what you can do and what you must do in a specific context.

**Template:**

> Summarize the rights and obligations of an individual [IN CONTEXT: e.g., as a tenant, as a consumer, as a contractor] in [JURISDICTION].
>
> Format as two clear lists:
> - Rights I Have: [Numbered list with brief explanations]
> - Obligations I Must Meet: [Numbered list with brief explanations]
>
> Also include:
> - Who Enforces These: Agency or body with authority
> - Where to Get Help: Official sources only
> - Common Pitfalls to Avoid: Mistakes people often make
>
> Add a "Quick Reference" card at the end - a 1-page summary I can keep handy.

**Example application:** Tenant rights, consumer protections, contractor responsibilities.

## Prompt 10: Step-by-Step Procedure

**Use when:** You need a complete walkthrough of a bureaucratic or legal process.

**Template:**

> Outline the step-by-step procedure for [PROCESS: e.g., filing a complaint, appealing a decision, registering a document] in [JURISDICTION].
>
> Assume I am an individual with no legal training.
>
> Include:
> - Prerequisites: What I need to have prepared before starting
> - Step-by-Step Instructions: Numbered list with estimated time for each
> - Forms Required: Names, numbers, and where to find them
> - Fees: Amounts and payment methods
> - Contact: Who to contact if stuck (agency/office with phone/email)
> - What to Do If Stuck: Escalation path
>
> Format: Printable checklist.

**Example application:** Filing taxes, applying for permits, appealing a decision, registering a business.

## Prompt 11: Personal Filing System

**Use when:** You need to organize your documents for fast retrieval.

**Template:**

> Design a simple, scalable personal filing system.
>
> For Digital Documents:
> - Folder Structure: Top-level categories + subfolders
> - Naming Convention: Consistent format for file names
> - Backup Schedule: Frequency and method
> - Retention Policy: What to keep and for how long
>
> For Physical Documents:
> - Binder/Accordion Organization: Categories and labels
> - Retention Schedule: Keep vs. shred by date
> - Offsite Storage: If applicable
>
> Goal: I want to locate any document within 2 minutes.
> Output: Printable "System Blueprint" with implementation checklist.

**Example application:** Home office organization, business document management, personal record-keeping.

## Prompt 12: Process Audit Framework

**Use when:** You need to analyze and improve any operational process.

**Template:**

> Design a process audit framework for [PROCESS: e.g., invoice payment, document signature, client onboarding].
>
> Include these steps:
> 1. Map Current Process: Inputs, actions, outputs (visual flowchart)
> 2. Identify Bottlenecks: Where do delays happen?
> 3. List Dependencies: People, systems, documents required
> 4. Compliance Checkpoints: Where could things go wrong?
> 5. Automation Opportunities: What could be automated?
>
> Output: A step-by-step audit guide I can apply to any process.
> Include a "Before/After" comparison section.

**Example application:** Invoice processing, client onboarding, content production, fulfillment workflows.

## Prompt 13: Contract Clause Review Checklist

**Use when:** You need to systematically review a legal agreement.

**Template:**

> Build a checklist for reviewing a contract clause in a [TYPE: e.g., service agreement, lease, NDA].
>
> For each clause, check for:
> - [ ] Clarity: Is it clear who does what?
> - [ ] Payment Terms: Amount, timing, method, late penalties
> - [ ] Termination: Conditions, notice period, penalties
> - [ ] Liability: Who is responsible for what?
> - [ ] Indemnification: Who covers legal costs if something goes wrong?
> - [ ] Dispute Resolution: How conflicts are handled
> - [ ] Renewal/Extension: Automatic or opt-in?
> - [ ] Unusual Language: Anything that stands out as unusual
>
> Output as a reusable template with key questions to ask about each element.
> Include a "Red Flag" indicator for elements that require professional review.

**Example application:** Service contracts, lease agreements, NDAs, partnership agreements.

## Prompt 14: Weekly Document Review Checklist

**Use when:** You need a routine for staying on top of administrative tasks.

**Template:**

> Create a weekly document review checklist for personal administration.
>
> Include these categories:
> - Incoming Documents: Mail, email attachments, digital uploads
> - Pending Actions: Responses needed, signatures required, deadlines approaching
> - Filing/Organization: Documents to file, archive, or shred
> - Follow-ups: Outstanding requests, unresolved issues
>
> Add time estimates for each category.
> Format: Printable checklist with checkboxes.
> Estimated total time: 20-30 minutes.
>
> Include a "Priority Rating" column: High/Medium/Low for each task.

**Example application:** Weekly administrative review, inbox maintenance, deadline tracking.

## Prompt 15: Email Thread Digest

**Use when:** You need a concise summary of a long email conversation.

**Template:**

> Summarize this email thread into a single-page digest:
>
> [PASTE EMAIL CHAIN HERE]
>
> Include:
> - Key Issue/Decision Point: What's at stake
> - Timeline: When each significant email was sent
> - Agreements or Commitments Made: What was promised by whom
> - Unresolved Items: What still needs to be addressed
> - Recommended Next Step: What should happen next
>
> Aim for 250-400 words. Use a timeline format where helpful.

**Example application:** Project status updates, dispute resolution summaries, decision documentation.

---

## Prompt 16: Notes Categorization

**Use when:** You have scattered notes that need organization.

**Template:**

> I have these notes on [TOPIC]:
>
> [PASTE NOTES HERE]
>
> Categorize them into groups under these headings:
> 1. Research & Resources: Things to read, save, or reference
> 2. Action Items: Things I need to do
> 3. Ideas: Speculative or brainstorming content
> 4. Questions to Investigate: Things I need to learn or confirm
> 5. Decisions Made: What I've already decided
>
> Within each category, summarize each note in one sentence.
> Flag duplicates for removal.

**Example application:** Meeting notes, research notes, brainstorming sessions.

---

## Prompt 17: Action Item Extraction

**Use when:** You need to pull specific tasks from a meeting or transcript.

**Template:**

> Extract all action items from this meeting/transcript:
>
> [PASTE TEXT HERE]
>
> For each action item, identify:
> - Action Statement: What needs to be done (clear, concise)
> - Owner: Who is responsible
> - Deadline: When it needs to be done (if mentioned)
> - Dependencies: What needs to happen first (if any)
> - Priority: High/Medium/Low
>
> Output as a bulleted list sorted by priority.
> Include a "Quick Wins" section for items that take less than 10 minutes.

**Example application:** Meeting follow-ups, project task lists, compliance action items.

## Prompt 18: Professional Closing Communication

**Use when:** Ending a project, relationship, or engagement.

**Template:**

> Draft a professional closing communication for [PROJECT/MATTER/RELATIONSHIP].
>
> Content:
> - Thank them for their work/assistance
> - Recap key outcomes or documents exchanged
> - Confirm no outstanding obligations (or list any)
> - Provide my final contact details
> - Offer to assist with transition (if relevant)
> - Important future dates (if any)
>
> Tone: Professional, appreciative, final but not abrupt.
>
> Format: Formal letter or email, depending on context.

**Example application:** Ending a contract, project completion, business relationship closure.

## Prompt 19: Friendly Follow-Up Email

**Use when:** You need to remind someone about an outstanding request.

**Template:**

> Draft a friendly follow-up email for an outstanding request.
>
> Original request sent on: [DATE]
> Subject: [SUBJECT]
> Original deadline: [DATE]
>
> What I need: [SPECIFIC INFORMATION/DOCUMENT]
> How I need it: [format, delivery method, etc.]
>
> Offer: [e.g., extension, assistance with format, alternative submission method]
>
> Politely ask if they need anything further from me.
> Tone: Friendly, professional, understanding. Not pushy.
>
> Subject line: "Following Up: [ORIGINAL SUBJECT]"

**Example application:** Pending documents, overdue responses, missed deadlines.

## Prompt 20: Disagreement Response

**Use when:** You need to professionally disagree with someone's statement or claim.

**Template:**

> I disagree with [STATEMENT/CLAIM] made by [RECIPIENT] regarding [SUBJECT].
>
> Draft a response that:
> - Acknowledges their perspective: "I understand you're saying..."
> - States my disagreement factually: "However, I understand it differently..."
> - Provides my reasoning: [EVIDENCE OR REASONING]
> - Proposes a path forward: [e.g., meeting, written exchange, review by a neutral party]
>
> Tone: Firm but respectful. No accusations. No "you are wrong" language.
>
> Include a request for a neutral review if the disagreement is significant.

**Example application:** Contract disputes, factual disagreements, interpretation differences.

---

## Prompt 21: Acknowledgment of Receipt

**Use when:** You need to confirm receipt of a document or package.

**Template:**

> Draft a brief letter acknowledging receipt of [DOCUMENT/ITEM/PACKAGE] from [SENDER].
>
> Details:
> - Document/Item Name: [NAME]
> - Date Received: [DATE]
> - Reference Number (if any): [NUMBER]
> - Condition (if applicable): [e.g., "in good condition," "appears complete"]
>
> State that I am reviewing the contents and will respond by [DATE].
> Tone: Professional, appreciative, action-oriented.
>
> Include my contact information for any questions.

**Example application:** Confirming document delivery, acknowledging package receipt, verifying submission.

**Template:**

> Draft a formal request for clarification regarding [DOCUMENT/COMMUNICATION/TERM].

> Context: [BRIEF BACKGROUND - 2-3 sentences]

## Prompt 23: Information Request Tracker

**Use when:** You need to track multiple outgoing information requests.

**Template:**

> Create a tracker for information requests I've sent out.
>
> Columns:
> - Request ID: Unique identifier
> - Date Sent: When I sent it
> - Recipient: Name and organization
> - Subject/Information Requested: Brief description
> - Deadline Given: When I asked for response
> - Follow-up Date: When to follow up if no response
> - Status: Sent, Pending, Received, Escalated, Closed
> - Notes: Key details, phone calls, emails
>
> Also include:
> - Weekly Review Dashboard: Overdue requests, due this week, completed
> - Follow-up Reminder: Draft text for follow-up emails
>
> Format: Database table view + dashboard summary.

**Example application:** Tracking document requests, information gathering, compliance inquiries.

## Prompt 24: Decision Log Entry

**Use when:** You need to document a key decision for future reference.

**Template:**

> Create a decision log entry for this decision:
>
> Decision Made: [WHAT]
> Date: [DATE]
> Context: [WHY THIS CAME UP - 2-3 sentences]
>
> Alternatives Considered:
> - [ALTERNATIVE 1] - Pro/Con summary
> - [ALTERNATIVE 2] - Pro/Con summary
> - [ALTERNATIVE 3] - Pro/Con summary (if applicable)
>
> Rationale for Selected Choice: [REASON - 2-3 sentences]
>
> Expected Impact: [SHORT-TERM] | [LONG-TERM]
> Review Date: [DATE] - When to check if this decision was correct
>
> Search Tags: [KEYWORDS for future retrieval]

**Example application:** Architectural decisions, strategic choices, policy decisions.

## Prompt 25: SOP Creation

**Use when:** You need to document a repeatable workflow.

**Template:**

> Create a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for my personal workflow:
>
> Title: [WORKFLOW NAME]
> Owner: [ME]
> Version: 1.0
> Frequency: [DAILY/WEEKLY/MONTHLY/AS NEEDED]
>
> Sections:
> 1. Purpose: Why this matters (one sentence)
> 2. Prerequisites: Tools, accounts, information needed
> 3. Step-by-Step Instructions: Numbered list with time estimates
> 4. Troubleshooting: What to do if X goes wrong
> 5. Related Documents: Links or file paths
> 6. Revision History: When and why this SOP was updated
>
> Write in clear, actionable language. Use short sentences.
> Include a "Quick Reference Card" version for fast execution.

**Example application:** Content production workflows, administrative routines, process documentation.
