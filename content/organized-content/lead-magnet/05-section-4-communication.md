# SECTION 4: COMMUNICATION (Prompts 16-20)

---

## PROMPT 16: Request for Clarification (Formal)

**Purpose:** Request formal clarification on a document, communication, or term.

**The Prompt:**
```
Draft a formal request for clarification regarding [DOCUMENT/COMMUNICATION/TERM].

Context: [BRIEF BACKGROUND - 2-3 sentences]

Specific questions I need answered:
1. [QUESTION 1 - be very specific]
2. [QUESTION 2]
3. [QUESTION 3 - if applicable]

State that I need clarification before I can proceed with [ACTION].
Request a response by [DATE] via [METHOD: email, letter, phone].

Tone: Professional, respectful. Frame as a reasonable request for understanding.
```

**Pro Tip:** Number your questions. This makes it easy for the recipient to answer each one directly.

---

## PROMPT 17: Acknowledgment of Receipt

**Purpose:** Formally acknowledge receipt of documents, items, or packages.

**The Prompt:**
```
Draft a brief letter acknowledging receipt of [DOCUMENT/ITEM/PACKAGE] from [SENDER].

Details:
- Document/Item Name: [NAME]
- Date Received: [DATE]
- Reference Number (if any): [NUMBER]
- Condition (if applicable): [e.g., "in good condition," "appears complete"]

State that I am reviewing the contents and will respond by [DATE].
Tone: Professional, appreciative, action-oriented.

Include my contact information for any questions.
```

**Pro Tip:** Date-stamp any document you receive. This establishes a clear record of when you got it.

## PROMPT 18: Non-Confrontational Disagreement

**Purpose:** Disagree with a claim or statement while maintaining a professional relationship.

**The Prompt:**
```
I disagree with [STATEMENT/CLAIM] made by [RECIPIENT] regarding [SUBJECT].

Draft a response that:
- Acknowledges their perspective: "I understand you're saying..."
- States my disagreement factually: "However, I understand it differently..."
- Provides my reasoning: [EVIDENCE OR REASONING]
- Proposes a path forward: [e.g., meeting, written exchange, review by a neutral party]

Tone: Firm but respectful. No accusations. No "you are wrong" language.

Include a request for a neutral review if the disagreement is significant.
```

**Pro Tip:** The phrase "I understand it differently" is one of the most powerful non-confrontational disagreement openers.

---

## PROMPT 19: Follow-up Reminder (Friendly)

**Purpose:** Send a friendly follow-up for an outstanding request.

**The Prompt:**
```
Draft a friendly follow-up email for an outstanding request.

Original request sent on: [DATE]
Subject: [SUBJECT]
Original deadline: [DATE]

What I need: [SPECIFIC INFORMATION/DOCUMENT]
How I need it: [format, delivery method, etc.]

Offer: [e.g., extension, assistance with format, alternative submission method]

Politely ask if they need anything further from me.
Tone: Friendly, professional, understanding. Not pushy.

Subject line: "Following Up: [ORIGINAL SUBJECT]"
```

**Pro Tip:** The best follow-up phrase: "I'm reaching out to make sure my request didn't get lost in the shuffle."

---

## PROMPT 20: Closing Communication

**Purpose:** Draft a professional closing communication for a project, matter, or relationship.

**The Prompt:**
```
Draft a professional closing communication for [PROJECT/MATTER/RELATIONSHIP].

Content:
- Thank them for their work/assistance
- Recap key outcomes or documents exchanged
- Confirm no outstanding obligations (or list any)
- Provide my final contact details
- Offer to assist with transition (if relevant)
- Important future dates (if any)

Tone: Professional, appreciative, final but not abrupt.

Format: Formal letter or email, depending on context.
```

**Pro Tip:** Keep a copy of every closing communication. It's your record that the matter was concluded.
